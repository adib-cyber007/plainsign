PlainSign explains wallet permissions before you sign them. A button labelled Free Mint can request permission to move an entire NFT collection. This presentation uses synthetic narration and shows the project entered by Mohamed Adib.

The problem is the gap between a website's promise and a wallet request's actual meaning. Transaction data can be difficult to read. Gasless signatures can also authorize asset movement later. This wallet image is a representative illustration.

PlainSign pauses supported requests before forwarding them to the wallet. It decodes the permission and shows a Safe, Caution or Danger verdict with explicit reasons. Reject stops the request using standard wallet error four thousand and one.

The separate product demo exercises the extension with an automated test wallet. A collection approval and a long lived Permit Two signature trigger Danger. A normal sign in and a known wrapped Ether request illustrate lower risk patterns. No real assets move.

The extension wraps supported injected Ethereum providers. Vee em decodes transactions and typed data. Public chain services enrich addresses. Open JSON rules determine the verdict. Continue forwards the original request unchanged. Optional simulation supports Sepolia only.

The product focuses on the consequence of a request, including signatures that can be used later. Every reason comes from inspectable rules. Optional artificial intelligence can reword text, but cannot decide the verdict and is disabled in the public package.

The goal is a clearer decision before wallet approval. There is no backend account or telemetry. These are heuristics, and a green verdict is not a safety guarantee. Provider interception is best effort. Independent security review remains a next step.

The September prototype predates this competition and is disclosed. October work adds Ethereum mainnet metadata integration, preserves known drainer evidence during R P C outages, and adds a sample walkthrough using the shipped decoder and rules. Sample metadata is labelled as simulated.

The consumer extension and decision rules stay free and open source. The proposed business is an integration package for wallet and application teams, with maintained rules and policy controls. Demand and pricing still need validation. No revenue or customers are claimed.

Initial distribution would focus on Ethereum communities and educators. The next experiment is to observe five users, measure whether they understand requests, and record false alarms and completion time. Seeking two integration design partners is a future target, not existing traction.
